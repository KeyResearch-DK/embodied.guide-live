Notes

AGIBOT's own site lists G2 as part of its “Genie” series alongside G1, describing it as an “Industrial-Grade Interactive Embodied Operation Robot”.

G2 moves on an omnidirectional wheeled chassis; the “knee” and “ankle” 2-DOF joints named in the spec sheet fold the torso through a height range of 1225-1795 mm rather than providing a walking gait.

Dual-arm payload is stated as 5 kg rated per arm (10 kg rated combined, 15 kg peak combined) - an arm figure, not a whole-body payload.

A per-joint degrees-of-freedom breakdown (head 3, arms 7 each x2, waist 3, knee/ankle 2) is given alongside a separate stated total of 25 excluding end effectors; the breakdown as given sums to 22, not 25. Both figures are the producer's own.

Dimensions, DOF, payload, sensor and compute figures come from AGIBOT's own G2 user manual document, a separate page from the main G2 product page.

PIA Automation offers a robot called I-BOT 2.0, which it says it launched jointly with AGIBOT on 16 October 2025; its datasheet gives the same weight (185 kg) and battery (1652 Wh), a runtime of 4+ hours, a height range of 123-180 cm and a chassis speed of 1.5 m/s.

AGIBOT's news release of 14 April 2026 says multiple G2 robots are integrated into the tablet production lines of Longcheer Technology.

AGIBOT presented G2 Max at WAIC 2026 on 18 July 2026 as its “first heavy-payload, force-controlled embodied task robot”. AGIBOT's store lists G2, G2 Air and G2 Max as the G2 series and says of the user manual: “The user manual describes G2. Coverage of G2 Air and G2 Max has not been confirmed.”

Shanghai Securities News reported that AGIBOT officially launched G2 Max on 26 October 2026, with arm figures of 20 kg per arm, 40 kg rated and up to 50 kg peak for both arms. The same report gives a vertical working range of 0.1 m to 2.5 m and a horizontal coverage of 1.2 m.
