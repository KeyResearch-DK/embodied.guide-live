How a figure reaches the page

  1. 01

    Fetched

    We fetch the manufacturer’s own page, datasheet or manual and keep the file as evidence, with its address and the date it was fetched.

  2. 02

    Recorded

    The figure goes into one of the schema’s 33 fields with a unit and a source.

  3. 03

    Checked for completeness

    The validator reads the whole catalogue again and stops the build if a figure is missing a unit, a source or a date.

  4. 04

    Built

    The pages are generated afresh from the same files, and the build runs the validator first every time.

The four states

A figure, a zero, a no and a gap are four different things, and each looks different on the page, so a zero is never shown as an empty cell and an empty cell is never read as a zero. Across the 559 robots, the manufacturers state 30 measured zeros and answer no 42 times.

The robot pages use these same marks. Each figure also carries a source mark (A, B, C …) that opens its source and note.

  • 14kg30.9lbA figure the manufacturer states.
  • 0A reported value of zero, not missing data.
  • NoExplicitly denied by the source.
  • Not statedNo published source states this field.

Technical details

Source marksShowHide

4,571 of the 4,598 stated fields carry a source mark. 3,522 point to the manufacturer’s own product page, 1,049 to a secondary source – a datasheet, a manual or developer documentation from the manufacturer, or a press report where the manufacturer gives no figure. The remaining 27 are a no given in place of a figure, which has nothing to source.

The steps in fullShowHide
Recorded
If the manufacturer does not state it, the field becomes not stated. It never becomes an estimate.
Checked for completeness
It checks that each figure is complete and sourced, not that the manufacturer’s claim is true; we do not test the robots. If the manufacturer’s metric and imperial figures do not match, we never correct them quietly: either the build stops, or the disagreement stands on the page beside the figure.
Built
That is why the site carries 0 figures without a source.
Where the pictures come fromShowHide

Pictures are the manufacturers’ and developers’ own photos, and press photos. For an AI model, a still from a video of the model at work may stand in. Where a background has been removed, the robot itself is unchanged.

No picture of a robot or a person is AI-generated. The one exception is a world or simulation model’s own output, which may appear on that model’s own page and is marked “Generated by the model”. Models without their own picture show a real photo of robot training or use, marked “Illustrative image — not this model”.

Robot AI modelsShowHide

Some entries are AI models, not robots. A model’s page describes the software; the robots it runs on have their own pages. A model’s type is the one its developer calls it – vision-language-action (VLA), world action model, world model, embodied reasoning or general-purpose model – and reads “Not stated” where the developer uses none of these words. A vision-language-action model takes camera images and an instruction and turns them into movements for a robot.

Figures on a model’s page come from its developer. A general-purpose model is included when its robot use is documented by the developer or by a named independent study; that study is a secondary source and shows only that the model has been used for robot tasks. The catalogue gives no benchmark results and no ranking of models.

Publisher

KeyResearch in Aarhus, Denmark, publishes the catalogue as a whole. It is not the source of any individual entry, and it is not a party to any sale.

For corrections or updated datasheets, use the form: Missing robot or wrong data? Corrections are verified at the source with an updated retrieval date.
